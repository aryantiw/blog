Youth: The Days of Questioning Everything

On the energy of youth, Swami Vivekananda, Nachiketa, and the power of asking better questions.

By Aryan TiwariJanuary 9, 2022
In this note#youth#questioning#vivekananda

Youth is the time in our lives when we are full of energy and can do almost any work without hesitation. Youth questions everything: science, politics, ideologies, and religion, finding its own way of life.

The idea of National Youth Day comes from the great Indian monk Swami Vivekananda, who was young when his life impacted the world. His speech at the World Parliament of Religions in Chicago continues to inspire generations.

Another story of youth comes from the ancient Indian text: a boy named Nachiketa was so determined to find the answers to life and the universe that he went to encounter death itself and made Death teach him the secrets of existence.

Swami Vivekananda famously said, “Give me ten boys like Nachiketa and I shall bring positive changes to this country.” This is the youth: a powerhouse. When directed positively, it can do seemingly impossible tasks. When left without direction, it can turn toward self-destruction.

What have these young people done so that their names are remembered by the world? Why cannot we be the next to become an inspiration?
